# Paired survey copy — response to Graham's prototype review

## Direction
Graham finds the paired layout more understandable. He wants more similar grammar
within each pair so attention falls on the difference, and asks whether “You”
would work better than “I”. This is qualitative feedback from one reviewer, not
proof of participant comprehension.

Recommend **I / my organisation / my colleagues**. These are statements participants
agree or disagree with about themselves. “You” would make the survey address the
participant, rather than let them endorse a statement. If tested, second person
should be consistent throughout; no mixed I/you experiment is proposed here.

Match repeated language where possible, while preserving different constructs.
Do not force all pairs into organisation versus individual: judgement, time,
recognition, meaning and security have other distinctions. Do not reverse the
existing statement-to-field mapping to make the visual pattern uniform.

## Proposed copy, in existing pair order
Both statements are shown below. “Retain” means keep the current wording, even
where full grammatical symmetry would change the question's meaning.

| Domain | First statement | Second statement | Review note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Judgement | When AI and I disagree, I rely on my own judgement. | I can recognise when AI output is wrong. | Retain: relying on judgement and recognising errors are different constructs, both about I. |
| Time and workload | When AI saves me time, I can use that time to improve my work. | When AI saves me time, I can use some of that time for myself, rather than taking on more work. | Parallel opening; preserve the work-quality versus personal-time distinction. |
| Working relationships | I speak with colleagues directly instead of relying on AI summaries of their work. | My colleagues speak with me directly instead of relying on AI summaries of my work. | Align “speak with”; preserve reciprocal direction and whose work is summarised. |
| Skill and craft | My organisation allows enough time to check the quality of AI-assisted work. | I check AI-assisted work as carefully as my other work. | Retain: time allowed and care exercised must stay distinct. Making both “check quality” would remove the comparison to other work. |
| Autonomy | I am comfortable explaining how I use AI at work. | My organisation trusts me to decide when to rely on AI output. | Expand contraction only; comfort explaining and discretion to rely are not mirror questions. Flag existing pair coherence for separate methodological review. |
| Learning | I have opportunities at work to develop skills that will remain useful as AI changes my work. | I actively develop skills that will remain useful as AI changes my work. | Align “develop skills”; preserve opportunity versus action. |
| Trust | I trust my colleagues to decide when to rely on AI output. | I am trusted to decide when to rely on AI output. | Align object and grammar; do not replace unspecified others with organisation. |
| Being seen | I give colleagues full credit when AI helps them work faster. | I receive credit for the judgement I add to AI-assisted work. | Retain: making both about speed or judgement would change the existing measures. |
| Meaning | As AI changes my tasks, I look for new ways to make my work meaningful. | The work I do alongside AI feels meaningful. | Retain: seeking meaning versus experiencing meaning. |
| Pace | The pace expected of me when I work with AI is sustainable. | I set a sustainable pace when I work with AI. | Align “when I work with AI”; expected versus personally set pace remains visible. |
| AI in team decisions | My organisation clearly explains how AI should be used. | I influence how my team uses AI. | Retain: organisational guidance versus individual influence. |
| Job security | I feel secure in my role as AI changes my work. | I am preparing for ways AI could change my role. | Retain: current security versus preparation, not organisation versus I. |

## Scope and acceptance
This is a proposed wording pass, not approved instrument wording. Six pairs have
candidate edits: time, relationships, autonomy (contraction only), learning, trust
and pace. All 24 originals remain in the live survey and existing prototypes.
Field identities, scores, answer options and completion rules are unchanged.

Before implementation, compare every edited statement against its original and
help text for meaning, actor, modality and timeframe. Do not claim unchanged
measurement or benchmark comparability from editorial judgement alone. Check any
relevant instrument rationale and record the impact of presentation order.

The paired-screen option changes the deliberate two-pass order. This remains an
explicit methodological decision for implementation; prototype preference does
not establish equivalence. Revision 2 excluded wording changes, so incorporate
the selected copy and order decision in a scope amendment before implementation.

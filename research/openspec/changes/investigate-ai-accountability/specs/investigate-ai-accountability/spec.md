# Investigate disclosure and accountability — specification revision 1

## Evidence
One participant identifies unclear disclosure of AI use and verification responsibility being passed to recipients. This is evidence of a possible coverage gap, not prevalence.

## Interpretation
Rationale v1.1 explicitly removes comfort explaining AI use. Current Quality and care measures checking time and comparative checking, not significant-use disclosure or who retains responsibility. The earlier proposal's references to existing openness questions are historical.

## Desired outcome
Establish whether disclosure and sender responsibility add distinct, comprehensible evidence before considering a future instrument change.

## Requirements
Compare candidate statements with the approved Quality and care and Guidance and influence wording. Use neutral single-idea statements in a research-only test; ask respondents to paraphrase disclosure and responsibility. Document overlap, ambiguities, role relevance and any burden.

## Exact wording or review prompts
Candidate A: I tell recipients when AI has made a significant contribution to work I share.
Candidate B: In my organisation, people who share AI-assisted work are responsible for checking its accuracy.
These are interview prompts for a future-version investigation, not approved scored questions.

## Constraints
Preserve approved v1.1 wording, historical item meanings, instrument versions, consent and privacy commitments. No participant data in repository artifacts. Use existing styling for later purpose-copy previews and maintain WCAG 2.2 AA. Drafting creates no participant-facing implementation.

## Assumptions
The two concepts may be distinct; disclosure does not establish verification quality, and reported organisational responsibility is not observed practice.

## Unknowns
What counts as significant contribution? How is responsibility understood for collaborative work? Is this a separate follow-up topic or worth replacing a scored item?

## Non-goals
Autonomous approval, live question additions or rewrites, new scoring or benchmark rules, releasing the paired preview, labelling respondents careless, and claiming population effects from individual comments.

Graham chooses investigation, deferral, redirection or closure. If approved, produce a cognitive-review worksheet automatically within this scope.

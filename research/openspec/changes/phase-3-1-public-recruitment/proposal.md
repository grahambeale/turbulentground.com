# Phase 3.1 public recruitment and referral — proposal revision 1

## Why

The current invite-only mechanism cannot test whether the research system can recruit participants through public discovery and participant referrals. Opening access without a reviewed identity, consent, attribution and governance model would weaken duplicate prevention, privacy commitments and research validity.

## Evidence

The Phase 3.1 brief defines the intended research loop and governance principles. Repository inspection confirms that the current participant journey is token-gated and invite-only, and that it lacks referral attribution, proposition identifiers and a reconstructable recruitment event model.

There is no observed evidence yet about willingness to share, effective propositions, representative recruitment, acceptable recognition, abuse levels or the number of observations required for reliable proposition comparison.

## Interpretation

Phase 3.1 should be treated as a governed recruitment study, not a viral-growth feature. The first release should establish trustworthy lineage from recruitment exposure to eligible completion while preserving consent, withdrawal, version provenance and human decision authority.

“Public” needs an explicit definition. A public landing page, public self-service identity issuance and unrestricted anonymous submission are different product and research choices.

## Desired outcome

A person can discover the research, make an informed choice to participate, complete the existing questionnaire, and optionally share an attributable invitation. The system can reconstruct recruitment paths and assess completion quality without exposing identities publicly or allowing agents to change the proposition autonomously.

## What changes

Proposed capabilities, each subject to the human decisions in `review.md`:

- public research discovery and controlled entry;
- server-issued pseudonymous participant and referral identifiers;
- first-touch recruitment and referral provenance;
- completion-triggered access to distinct share hypotheses;
- an event model from landing through eligible completion;
- private referral feedback, if approved;
- research-quality and data-quality analysis;
- agent proposals with explicit human governance;
- traceability from evidence through decision and OpenSpec.

## Capabilities

The specification defines observable requirements for:

1. eligibility and public identity issuance;
2. source and referral attribution;
3. event integrity and metric definitions;
4. sharing and referral-chain behavior;
5. privacy, consent, withdrawal and retention;
6. abuse, duplicates and data-quality flags;
7. agent analysis boundaries;
8. measurable pilot readiness and acceptance.

It does not select layouts, final copy, components, storage technology or deployment architecture.

## Impact

Potential benefits are a larger evidence pool, measurable recruitment learning and a complete demonstration of the agent-led research loop.

Material risks are self-selection bias, referral-cluster bias, accidental identity disclosure, attribution errors, gaming, bot traffic, misleading proposition “winners”, consent mismatch, and scope expansion into a public-site redesign. These risks require the human decision register before design begins.

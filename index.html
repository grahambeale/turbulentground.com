    The AI shift,seen from the ground.

    From a distance, AI at work looks like one story. Up close, it is a different one for everyone living through it. This independent study collects those stories, so we can see what is really changing and learn from each other.

    The view from orbit

    From up here, it looks like momentum.

    New tools every month. Pilots announced, licences bought, strategies rewritten. From a distance, every organisation sits somewhere on the same curve, heading the same way.

    It is a tidy picture. It is also drawn from a very long way up.

    Closer in

    Down here, it is personal.

    Zoom in and the curve breaks into people, making small decisions every day.

    • A designer deciding whether to trust what the model produced.
    • An engineer who spent a saved hour fixing something that had bothered them for months.
    • A manager reading the summary instead of the colleague who wrote the work.
    • A researcher with more time to think than she has had in years.

    Some of it is going well. Some of it isn't. Most of it goes unrecorded.

    Why this study exists

    Product teams and the organisations around them still have real blind spots about how to make AI work well for people. The ones most likely to see those blind spots are the people doing the work.

    What the study asks

    Twelve themes. Your experience of each.

    You read 24 short statements, two for each theme, and say how far each one matches your working life. It isn't a test, and there are no right answers.

    • 01Judgement
    • 02Time and workload
    • 03Working relationships
    • 04Quality and care
    • 05Autonomy
    • 06Learning
    • 07Trust
    • 08Being seen
    • 09Meaning
    • 10Pace
    • 11Guidance and influence
    • 12Job security
    An example pair: Trust

    I trust my colleagues’ judgement when they use AI.

    My colleagues trust my judgement when I use AI.

    For people working in or alongside product development teams, and the wider organisations around them.

    What you get back

    Your answers, returned to you.

    • A personal summary

      At the end you see a summary of your own answers, keeping two things apart: what you contribute, and the conditions around you.

    • A benchmark, once it is fair

      When enough people have taken part for the comparison to mean something, you can see how your answers compare with the study as a whole. Not before.

    • What the study finds

      I write openly about the findings. If you choose, I will email you occasional updates, no more than once every 30 days.

    Your data

    Private by design.

    • Stored apartYour name and email are kept separately from your answers.
    • Never named without askingIndividual responses stay private. I would only quote you by name after asking you about that specific quote.
    • At your paceUp to 14 days to finish. One reminder after seven. Unfinished answers are deleted after 14 days.
    • RegisteredA personal research project, registered with the Information Commissioner’s Office (ZC232927). For people aged 18 or over.
